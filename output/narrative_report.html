Chapter 3 · How the Book Is Aging

The portfolio is seasoning, not just growing

Even as new cohorts keep originating, the balance mix is shifting toward older, more-matured vintages — the existing book ages into "seasoned" faster than new originations can dilute it.

Chapter 4 · What Cohorts Reveal

Per-account economics mature on a predictable curve

Every vintage starts negative (acquisition costs land before revenue does), climbs through its first several quarters, then plateaus. Older and newer cohorts of the same merchant follow essentially the same shape at the same age — a sign the underlying unit economics are stable, not drifting.

Merchant 1 shown, three representative vintages by age at booking. The newest vintage's later quarters are forecast, not yet observed.

Chapter 5 · The Reveal

LTV/CAC is inverted by FICO tier — sharply

Better credit tiers are less profitable to acquire, not more. High-FICO customers spend heavily — driving proportionally more rewards cost, since rewards scale with spend — but revolve less, generating far less interest income, the portfolio's largest revenue line. Lower-FICO customers carry higher charge-off rates, but a materially higher APR/yield on what they do revolve more than compensates.

Chapter 6 · Winners and Drags

Scale and unit-economics efficiency aren't the same thing

Merchant 4 is the strongest contributor ($41.9M Contribution Profit, 19.5% margin) over the forecast window. Merchant 7 is the one clear structural drag (-$11.7M, -7.0% margin) — not just smaller or slower-growing, genuinely negative.

Chapter 7 · The Bottom Line

Portfolio LTV/CAC: 0.82x

Below breakeven on a blended basis — driven almost entirely by the FICO-tier dynamic in Chapter 5, not by any single merchant. Several individual merchants and every below-Good FICO tier clear well above 1x; the blend is dragged down by acquisition spend on segments that don't pay it back within three years.

Risk 1 · Thin-history extrapolation

The two newest merchants have only 2–4 quarters of actual history. Their forecasts lean on pooled cross-merchant patterns rather than their own observed behavior.

Risk 2 · FICO-tier reward economics may be mispriced

The mechanism is real and traceable in the data, but this is a portfolio-wide average — worth validating against the actual unit-economics view before acting on it.

Risk 3 · Every rate is held flat

Yield, loss rate, interchange, rewards — none carry a macro or competitive-response assumption into the 2-year horizon.
